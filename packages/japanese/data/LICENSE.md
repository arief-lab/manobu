# JLPT vocabulary + grammar corpus — provenance & license

The files `n1.json` … `n5.json` in this directory are generated by
`scripts/fetch-vocab.ts`, which joins two upstream sources. The files in
`data/grammar/` come from a separate upstream (below).

## Chain of rights

### 1. The JLPT word lists

- **Original data**: the JLPT word lists published at
  <https://www.tanos.co.uk/jlpt/>, licensed **Creative Commons BY** by
  **Jonathan Waller** — contributes `word`, `reading`, `level`.
- **Conversion**: `Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary` converts those lists to JSON
  and is licensed **MIT** (Copyright (c) 2026 Bluskyo). See
  <https://github.com/Bluskyo/JLPT_Vocabulary>.

### 2. English meanings & parts of speech

- **Source**: [JMdict/EDICT](https://www.edrdg.org/jmdict/jmdict.html)
  (© The Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group), distributed

- **Conversion**: `scriptin/jmdict-simplified` publishes JMdict as JSON
  (release assets on <https://github.com/scriptin/jmdict-simplified>,
  pinned at fetch time) — contributes `meaning` and `partOfSpeech`.

## What we ship

For each JLPT level (N5–N1) a JSON array of entries:

- `{ word, reading, level }` — the surface form (kanji or kana), the
  hiragana reading, and the level. Multiple readings of the same word are
  preserved as separate entries, and kana-only variants appear as their own
  words (matching the source).
- `meaning` — English glosses from JMdict (deduped, capped at 8), present
  for ~99.7% of entries.
- `partOfSpeech` — coarse POS bucket (noun/verb/adjective/…), when JMdict
  provides one.

Because JMdict is **share-alike (CC BY-SA 4.0)**, the `meaning` /
`partOfSpeech` data in these files, and any redistribution of them, is
covered by CC BY-SA 4.0. See <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/>.

### 3. Grammar points & conjugations

- **Source**: [`jkindrix/japanese-language-data`](https://github.com/jkindrix/japanese-language-data)
  (Copyright (c) 2026 Justin Kindrix and contributors), licensed **CC BY-SA 4.0**.
  - `data/grammar/n1.json` … `data/grammar/n5.json` — 595 hand-curated grammar
    points (patterns, meanings, formations, examples), all `review_status: draft`.
  - `data/grammar/conjugations.json` — 3,511 verb/adjective conjugation tables
    auto-generated from JMdict entries (CC-BY-SA 4.0, per its embedded metadata).

  Download pin: `main` branch of the upstream repo at fetch time. Because it is
  **share-alike**, the grammar/conjugation data and any redistribution of it is
  covered by CC BY-SA 4.0.

### 4. Kanji & pitch accent

- **Source**: same upstream,
  [`jkindrix/japanese-language-data`](https://github.com/jkindrix/japanese-language-data)

  - `data/kanji.json` — 13,108 kanji entries derived from **KANJIDIC2**
    (via `scriptin/jmdict-simplified`): readings (on/kun/nanori), English
    meanings, stroke count, JLPT (Waller + legacy), grade, frequency,
    radical.
  - `data/pitch-accent.json` — 136,796 pitch-accent entries with per-entry

  情報検索 re-stamp below), split by upstream paper. `data/reading/n5.json` is

## 12. reading/n4.json — the composed masked-slot route (TASK-173.9.6), replaced by authored prose (TASK-173.9.19)

- **STATUS**: `data/reading/n4.json` no longer contains these rows. It ships 16
  authored comprehension rows under §20, because a cloze over one sentence is
  vocabulary-in-context printed under a 内容理解 heading, and the section below
  is the measurement that proved no licensable source could supply the connected
  prose a real 短文 is. The masked-slot contract stays implemented and tested in
  `check:reading-n4` (the batch this section describes is in the repo's judge
  history), and `gen:reading-n4` now exits 1 rather than overwrite the judged file.
- **What it was**: 24 N4 rows GENERATED here, not vendored. `scripts/gen-reading-n4.ts`
  screens every Tatoeba sentence to all-N5+N4 vocabulary (or a function word),
  blanks ONE noun that occurs exactly once in it, and writes the row. The
  passage is therefore that sentence with one word replaced by `＿＿`; the
  answer option is the word the source sentence itself prints.
- **Why it is not the same product as §11**: a §11 row is authentic test
  material. A row here is a cloze whose key is mechanical — it needs no reading
  comprehension of a multi-sentence discourse, only of one sentence — because
  the longest fully-in-level Tatoeba sentence at N1 is 46 runes and a real 短文
  is 150–400. It is labelled `provenance: "composed"`.
- **Text rights are unchanged**: each row carries verbatim licensed Japanese
  prose from **Tatoeba, CC BY 2.0 FR** (the sentence id is in `sourceIds`), and
  the Tanaka dictionary that segments it is public domain. Redistribution must
  carry the same Tatoeba attribution as §8. `stem` is one fixed Japanese line
  authored here (the blank-filling instruction); `sourceEn` is the Tatoeba
  English gloss, kept only so the batch can be eyeballed by a human reviewer.
- **What the English-side screens rule out**: an answer no word of the source
  translation names, and a distractor that is another English label for the
  answer (same dominant JMdict sense). Both are checked by `check:reading-n4`,
  not asserted here. Measured effect: the translation screen removes about 60%
  of otherwise-valid candidate slots (423 -> 169 on a 7,386-sentence sample);
  the sense screen removes 346 noun pairs corpus-wide, of which none happened
  to appear in the shipped batch - it is insurance against hand-edited rows
  and future vocabulary growth, not a filter doing work today.
- **What the corpus-context screen rules out** (added with the same insurance
  framing): a distractor some OTHER in-level sentence fills the SAME context
  frame with (「雨が降っている」 vs 「雪が降っている」 share the frame
  `が|降っている`, so a reader who knows the frame could argue the distractor
  in). `buildContextFillers` indexes every in-level sentence by its other
  linked surfaces; the generator rejects such distractors before the draw and
  the gate  re-proves it as `distractor-corpus-plausible`. Measured: it fires
  ZERO times on the shipped 24-row batch, and corpus-wide 638 of 24,224
  context keys hold more than one filler (に|行く -> 旅行/食事/私/どこ) -
  real insurance, not a filter doing work today.
- **Solvability and the freeze-time judge** (added after the first batch was
  judged): the server never serves `sourceEn`, so a row is only fair when its
  frame is decidable from the Japanese the learner sees. The independent-model
  blind judge (`prompts/reading-validator.md`, opencode/space-bunny-free, key
  withheld) marked 13 of the first 24 rows AMBIGUOUS/UNANSWERABLE under that
  condition. Two deterministic screens now encode the failure mode
  (`isContextSolvable` -> `answer-context-ambiguous`: unique corpus frame + a
  content word in the frame) and the judge's verdicts are frozen as data
  (`data/reading/n4-rejected.json` -> `row-judge-rejected`): the generator
  skips rejected ids, the gate refuses them, and CI never runs a model. The
  batch was regenerated; one row (n4m-78489) was replaced.
- **Why the passages stay single-sentence** (measured, not assumed): a real
  短文 is connected prose, and the one CC-suitable source large enough to hold
  it, Japanese Wikipedia (CC BY-SA 4.0, rightsinfo-verified via the MediaWiki
  API), was probed for feasibility. Byte-range fetches of 27 everyday articles
  (multistream dump, one article per bzip2 block, no 4.9 GB download) found
  ZERO paragraphs of 150-400 runes at >= 0.8 N5+N4 vocabulary coverage; the
  best was 0.69, and the top scorers were table rows and ref lists, not prose.
  Encyclopedic Japanese is too dense with proper nouns and technical
  vocabulary to yield N4 passages at any scale this pipeline could trust, so
  the single-sentence cloze stays and the gap is honestly labelled.
